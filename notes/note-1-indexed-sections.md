# P.L. 119-21 in 58 sections of Title 26: instruction by instruction

Arxo Law · October 2026 · contact: hi@arxo.io

## Summary

We took every amending instruction of P.L. 119-21 that touches 58 inflation-indexed
sections of Title 26 — 65 instructions, 77 elementary edits — and applied it to the Code
as it stood before enactment. We then compared the result with later consolidations by
the Office of the Law Revision Counsel (OLRC).

- **Execution.** 63 instructions execute exactly as written. One quotes text that is not
  in the Code; one matches two places where it names one.
- **Cross-references.** Internal cross-references in the same sections were resolved
  mechanically against the consolidated text, and the unresolved forms were read by hand.

Items 1–4 below bear on how the law operates. Items 5 and 6 are textual discrepancies
without an identified effect on current amounts. Item 7 needs no action. The note
separates what the text establishes from questions that need further legal analysis.

## 1. Refundable adoption credit: two section lists were not conformed

- **Text.** §70402(a) (139 Stat. 213) adds §23(a)(4): “So much of the credit allowed under
  paragraph (1) as does not exceed $5,000 shall be treated as a credit allowed under
  subpart C and not as a credit allowed under this subpart.” The $5,000 is indexed after
  2025 by §23(h)(3).
- **Treatment as an overpayment.** Because the portion is treated as allowed under
  subpart C, the excess over tax is an overpayment under §6401(b)(1) and is refunded under
  §6402. The preamble to REG-119882-25 describes this route for the adoption credit.
  No amendment to §6401 is needed.
- **Two provisions that name sections one by one are unchanged.**
  - 26 U.S.C. 6211(b)(4)(A) treats as negative amounts of tax, for deficiency purposes,
    the excess of credits under “sections 21 by reason of subsection (g) thereof, 24 by
    reason of subsections (d) and (i)(1) thereof, 25A by reason of subsection (i)
    thereof, 32, 34, 35, 36, 36B, 6428, 6428A, 6428B, and 7527A”. Section 23 is not
    listed.
  - 31 U.S.C. 1324(b)(2) allows disbursement from the permanent refund appropriation for
    refunds due from credit provisions “enacted before January 1, 1978, or enacted by the
    Taxpayer Relief Act of 1997”, or from listed sections “21, 24, 25A, 35, 36, 36A,
    36B, …, 7527A”.
    - Section 23 was added in 1996 (P.L. 104-188 §1807). The Taxpayer Relief Act of 1997
      amended it but did not enact it, and subsection (a)(4) dates from 2025.
    - Section 23 is not in the list.
- **Precedents.** Each time Congress made one of these credits refundable, in whole or in
  part, it named the section in both lists:
  - §25A, made partly refundable by P.L. 111-5 §1004(b)(7) and (8) (2009);
  - the adoption credit itself, moved into subpart C as §36C, by P.L. 111-148
    §10909(b)(2)(N) and (P) (2010);
  - §21 and §24, made refundable and fully refundable, respectively, for 2021, by P.L.
    117-2 §9631(c) and §9611(b)(4).

  In 2021 Congress added “24” to 31 U.S.C. 1324(b)(2), although §24 itself was enacted
  by the Taxpayer Relief Act of 1997. The refundable rule then added, §24(i), was not
  enacted by that Act. On the same reading, §23(a)(4), added in 2025, is outside the
  1997 clause.
- **Question.** Two points need an answer:
  - how a deficiency attributable to the refundable portion is determined under §6211;
  - which part of 31 U.S.C. 1324(b) covers the portion paid in excess of liability.

  The refundable portion is claimed on Form 1040, line 30 (Form 8839 instructions, 2025).
- **Possible correction**, effective as if included in §70402:
  - (i) in §6211(b)(4)(A), insert “23 by reason of subsection (a)(4) thereof,” after “21
    by reason of subsection (g) thereof,”;
  - (ii) in 31 U.S.C. 1324(b)(2), insert “23,” after “section 21,”. The prospective
    amendment of that list by P.L. 117-328 should be taken into account.
- **JCT.** JCS-1-26 (pp. 128–129) describes the refundable portion and its indexation
  ($5,120 for 2026) but does not address §6211(b)(4)(A) or 31 U.S.C. 1324(b)(2).

## 2. §23(c)(1): one strike-and-insert, two occurrences

- **Text.** §70402(c) (139 Stat. 214): “striking ‘credit allowable under subsection (a)’
  and inserting ‘portion of the credit allowable under subsection (a) which is allowed
  under this subpart’”.
  - The phrase appears twice in §23(c)(1): in the limit test and in the amount carried
    forward.
  - The instruction does not say “each place it appears”, a phrase the Act uses
    elsewhere.
- **Consolidation history.** OLRC release 119-33 made the substitution in the second place
  only. Release 119-73not60 made it in both places, “to reflect the probable intent of
  Congress”.
- **Effect.** Read the first way, the refundable portion already paid out still enters
  the carryforward test. The IRS does not administer it that way. The Line 18 example in
  the 2025 Form 8839 instructions computes the carryforward as $17,280 − $10,000 −
  $5,000 = $2,280. The defect is in the text, not in current practice.
- **JCT.** JCS-1-26 (p. 129, n. 674) states that “a clarification may be desirable to
  carry out this intent unambiguously” and that OLRC’s compilation incorporates such a
  clarification. We document the change between OLRC releases 119-33 and 119-73not60.
- **Possible correction.** Insert “each place it appears” in §70402(c).

## 3. §529A(b)(2)(B): the quoted text does not exist

- **Text.** §70204(a)(2)(A) (139 Stat. 185) inserts the ABLE rollover exception after
  “except as provided in the case of contributions under subsection (c)(1)(C)”.
  Since 2014 the provision has read “except in the case of contributions under
  subsection (c)(1)(C)”.
- **Consolidation.** OLRC executed the insertion after the existing phrase “to reflect the
  probable intent of Congress”.
- **Possible correction.** Amend §70204(a)(2)(A) by striking “except as provided in the
  case of” and inserting “except in the case of”, effective as if included in the Act.

## 4. Senior deduction and the non-itemizer rules

- **Text.** §70103(a)(3) (139 Stat. 159) adds §151(d)(5)(C): “there shall be allowed a
  deduction in an amount equal to $6,000”.
  - Section 151(a) allows “the exemptions provided by this section” as deductions.
  - Section 63(b)(2) admits for non-itemizers “the deduction for personal exemptions
    provided in section 151”.
  - Section 63(d) treats as itemized every deduction other than those allowed in arriving
    at adjusted gross income and those referred to in §63(b). Section 62 does not mention
    §151.
- **Why it matters.** The same Act added its other new non-itemizer deductions (§§224,
  225 and 163(h)(4)) to §63(b)(5)–(7). The senior deduction was placed in §151 with no
  corresponding entry. Unless it is part of the deduction for personal exemptions, the
  text reads as making it an itemized deduction.
- **Heading.** The heading of §70103 — “Termination of deduction for personal exemptions
  other than temporary senior deduction” — treats the new deduction as part of the
  deduction for personal exemptions, and may be the basis on which the IRS allows it.
  The operative text does not say so, and headings are not operative text.
- **Administration.** The IRS allows it whether or not the taxpayer itemizes
  (Schedule 1-A; 2025 Form 1040 instructions). The 2025 Instructions for Form 6251 state
  that the deduction “is treated as a personal exemption that is added back to alternative
  minimum taxable income”; they cite §56(b)(5)(D), which does not exist — the provision is
  §56(b)(1)(D).
- **JCT.** JCS-1-26 (p. 8) states that the senior deduction “is allowed to taxpayers who
  claim the standard deduction and to taxpayers who elect to itemize deductions”. Its
  n. 26 states that a technical correction may be necessary to clarify that the senior
  deduction is treated as a personal exemption. Such a correction would match current IRS
  practice for both the standard deduction and the AMT.
- **Possible correction.** In §63(b)(2), insert “(including the deduction under section
  151(d)(5)(C))” after “section 151”.

## 5. References to the former §68(b)

§70111(a) (139 Stat. 164–165) amended §68 in full. New §68(b) (“Coordination with other
limitations”) has no paragraphs and does not define an applicable amount. Five references,
in four provisions, remain:

- §1(f)(7)(A): “section 68(b)(2)”;
- §151(d)(3)(A) and (B): “the applicable amount in effect under section 68(b)”;
- §164(b)(5)(H)(ii)(III): “the applicable amount (as determined under section 68(b))”;
- §642(b)(2)(C)(i)(I): “section 68(b)(1)(C)”.

OLRC flags each with a References in Text note. We have not established a numerical effect
in the calculations we reviewed. Form 1041-ES (2026) gives the qualified disability trust
exemption as $5,300 and states that it “is not subject to phaseout”.

**Possible correction.** Strike the references, or replace them where a threshold is still
intended; §164(b)(5)(H) still uses its reference for the sales tax tables.

## 7. Checked, no action needed

- **§63(b)(6).** §70203(b) (139 Stat. 177) inserted “and” without a comma, and OLRC
  marked it “So in original”. P.L. 119-108 §2(c)(1) has since replaced it with a comma.
- **§199A(i)(3).** The final period printed at 139 Stat. 162 was missing in OLRC releases
  119-33 and 119-73not60. Release 119-121 has it.
- **§461(l)(1).** §70601(a) strikes “and before January 1, 2029,”. That text exists only
  after the prospective amendment by P.L. 117-169 §13903(b), which substitutes “2029” for
  the “2027” inserted by P.L. 117-2. §70601(a) applies from the same date: taxable years
  beginning after December 31, 2026. It executes correctly.

## Method and limits

- **Execution.** Each instruction was written down as an explicit edit and applied to
  the pre-enactment text, counting occurrences. Where the count differs from the
  instruction, the edit is not made and the alternatives are listed. The result was
  compared with the later OLRC releases.
- **Cross-references.** 17 reference forms were left unparsed by the automatic resolver.
- **Effective dates.** Effective-date transitions were not tested one by one; §461
  was tested.
- **Checking.** Every item drawn from P.L. 119-21 was checked by hand against the
  Statutes at Large page.
